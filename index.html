SCIENTIFIC BACKGROUND

Science & Sources

LIFELOOP is a proposed integrated Mars settlement concept. These NASA resources informed the scientific background of its systems. They are references, not live data feeds in this prototype.

NASA MOXIE · Oxygen production

NASA reports that MOXIE produced 122 grams of oxygen in total. Its best rate was 12 grams per hour at 98% purity or better. These measured results inspired our oxygen-generation concept.

View NASA MOXIE results · Read NASA’s MOXIE mission report

NASA OSDR · Plant research dataset

We reference OSD-217, a NASA study of Arabidopsis plants grown aboard the International Space Station. It investigates plant growth and molecular changes in the spaceflight environment. It is relevant to our greenhouse concept, but it is not data about the exact crops shown in LIFELOOP.

Open NASA dataset OSD-217 · About NASA OSDR

NASA DONKI · Latest space-weather update (13 November 2026)

DONKI event 2026-11-13T03:24:00-CME-001 was reported as a C-type coronal mass ejection with an estimated speed of about 525 km/s and a 17° half-angle. The related CCMC notification estimated a minor impact at OSIRIS-APEX and glancing blows at Europa Clipper and Parker Solar Probe. This particular event was not reported as a direct Mars impact. A separate weekly report issued on 11 November described a 9 November CME with a predicted glancing blow at Mars on 13 November. These are space-weather analyses/model estimates, not measurements of radiation at the Martian surface.

LEARNING CENTER

What can you learn?

Explore the colony to discover how science and engineering can help people live in a challenging environment like Mars.

01 · LIFE SUPPORT

Learn why people need oxygen, clean water, and reliable food, and how the colony's systems help provide them.

02 · OXYGEN FROM MARS

Discover how the MOXIE experiment demonstrated oxygen production from carbon dioxide in the Martian atmosphere.

03 · PLANTS & ALGAE

Explore how photosynthesis uses light, water, and carbon dioxide. Learn about the proposed roles of Spirulina and Chlorella in a biological life-support system.

04 · WATER & SUSTAINABILITY

Understand why water recovery, recycling, and careful resource use are important when supplies are limited.

05 · ENERGY & SPACE WEATHER

Learn why solar power matters and why crews monitor solar activity. NASA DONKI records help introduce space-weather events, but they are not direct radiation measurements on Mars.

06 · ENGINEERING CONNECTIONS

See how oxygen, water, food, energy, and crew safety depend on one another. A change in one system can affect the whole settlement.

07 · DECISIONS & CONSEQUENCES

Each station offers strategy choices. Saving energy may reduce oxygen production later; intensive recycling can leave less power for the greenhouse. Your final colony condition depends on the choices you make.

Walk within range of a station and press E once to open it. Choose one of the two strategies with the left mouse button. Each choice changes resources immediately and advances one mission day, causing ongoing crew needs to consume supplies. Press E a second time to show NASA references and source links. In a greenhouse, left-click a crop to plant it; planting uses water and energy and improves the food reserve. Your starting conditions change between playthroughs. For crew houses, press E near the entrance to enter or leave. EXIT returns to the main menu and resets the mission. The thick regolith roof represents radiation shielding.
